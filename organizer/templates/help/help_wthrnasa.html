NASA POWER Weather Data

You can obtain the weather data from the NASA POWER web site. This site contains simulated weather data (based from actual measurements) for any locations in the world, and this option can be useful when the weather data for your location is incomplete or missing.

To obtain the weather data from the NASA POWER web site:

  1. Go to the Weather Files page, and click on the Upload button.
  2. Now click on the Get from NASA POWER panel.
  3. Here you will specify the location of your oil palm site. You can either type in the GPS latitude and longitude coordinates (in decimal degrees), or you can click on the map to select your site.
  4. Option 1. Enter the exact latitude and longitude (in decimal degrees) of your oil palm site, then fill in the starting year and duration for the weather data. The starting year must be 1981 or later. For instance, if you require weather data from 1990 to 2010, then fill in 1990 in the Start year field and 21 in the No. of years field (∵ 1990 to 2010 spans 21 years). Give your oil palm site a name in the Site name field.
  5. Option 2. Instead of entering the exact latitude and longitude of your oil palm site, you could click on the map to select your site. To do this, click on the Pin your site checkbox to enable site location selection via the map. On your desktop, you can pan the map by clicking (and holding the mouse key) then dragging your mouse cursor over the map. On your smartphone, panning is done with two fingers. You could also zoom in or out of the map. Once you have selected your site, you can adjust (i.e., fine tune) your site's GPS location via the Lat. and Long. fields (see Fig. 4). Enter the start year and duration for the required weather data, and the site name (see Option 1 above).
  6. In this example, we have chosen a site near the town Taiping, with the GPS location: 4.5676 °N and 101.0024 °E. The weather data is from 1981 to 2000 (20 years). Clicking on the Retrieve NASA weather button will retrieve the weather data from the NASA POWER website and upload the data to PySawit Web.

    If your site is in Malaysia (as in this example), it is recommended that you tick the check box to calibrate NASA's data. Comparisons with measured Malaysian weather have shown that NASA's weather data require calibration to better agree with measured weather, particularly with rainfall.
  7. Once uploaded, the retrieved weather data will be displayed in a tabular form. Charts will also be displayed to show the distribution of the weather parameters. In addition, statistical characteristics of the weather parameters will also be shown in a series of tables.
  8. The newly uploaded weather data from the NASA POWER web site will now be listed in your Weather Files page. This weather file is now ready to be used in your crop simulations.
